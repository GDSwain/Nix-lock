* Project Overview

Most software update systems optimize for receiving the newest available version. Nix-Lock instead optimizes for deploying a previously selected version only after it has had time to prove itself in the real world.

Nix-Lock is a deployment workflow for NixOS.

Rather than deploying the latest available NixOS revision immediately, Nix-Lock captures an immutable revision at a chosen point in time, preserves that revision unchanged for a configurable observation period, and then intentionally deploys that exact revision.

The project exists to introduce a deliberate delay between *capture* and *deployment* while preserving the normal NixOS user experience.

* Background

The project originated from an investigation into whether the publication age of a NixOS channel could be used as a "cooldown timer" before updating.

The original hypothesis was that waiting until the current channel had existed for a defined period would reduce the likelihood of deploying problematic updates.

Investigation showed that this approach was fundamentally flawed.

The publication timestamp describes the age of the current channel head. Because channel heads move whenever a newer revision is promoted, the timer continually resets. It therefore cannot measure the maturity of any particular NixOS revision.

The investigation was valuable because it identified the wrong metric and led to a clearer understanding of the real problem.

** Problem Statement

Current update workflows generally answer the question:

> "What is the latest revision available today?"

Nix-Lock asks a different question:

> "Which previously captured revision has existed long enough to justify deploying?"

These are not equivalent.

The goal is not to delay updates for their own sake.

The goal is to separate the act of selecting a revision from the act of deploying it.

** Project Aim

Capture an immutable NixOS revision.

Preserve that revision unchanged for a configurable observation period.

Deploy that exact revision at a later date.

Continue normal day-to-day use of the system throughout the observation period.

** Design Principles

- Simplicity before features.
- Policy before implementation.
- Preserve existing NixOS workflows wherever possible.
- Require the minimum practical migration.
- Introduce no unnecessary complexity.
- Prefer existing Nix mechanisms over custom solutions.
- Make deployment intentional rather than automatic.
- Record decisions using Git wherever practical.



* Scope

** Nix-Lock is responsible for:

*** Capturing a specific NixOS revision.
*** Separating revision selection from deployment.
*** Preserving that revision unchanged.
*** Recording the candidate for later deployment.
*** Supporting an intentional deployment workflow.
*** Providing reproducibility.
*** Supporting configurable observation periods.

* Non-Goals

** Nix-Lock is not intended to:

*** Replace NixOS generations.
*** Replace backups.
*** Snapshot user data.
*** Restore a complete machine to an earlier state.
*** Predict whether updates are safe.
*** Test packages.
*** Replace Git.
*** Replace existing NixOS tooling.
*** Require Home Manager.
*** Require users to redesign an existing modular NixOS configuration.

* Guiding Philosophy

Nix-Lock is not an update manager.

It is a release management workflow.

The project is concerned only with deciding *when* a captured NixOS revision should become the running system.

Everything else remains ordinary NixOS administration.

* Terminology

**Revision**

An immutable upstream NixOS/Nixpkgs state.

**Capture**

Recording a specific revision for future deployment.

**Candidate**

A captured revision currently undergoing observation.

**Observation Period**

The time between capture and deployment.

**Deploy**

Building and activating the captured revision.

**Current**

The revision presently running on the system.

**Latest**

The newest revision currently available upstream.

* Conceptual Workflow

1. Capture today's revision.
2. Continue using the computer normally.
3. Allow the captured revision to remain under public observation.
4. Deploy that exact revision.
5. Capture the next candidate.
6. Repeat.

* User Experience

Normal operation should remain unchanged.

Nix-Lock is intended to change the timing of system deployment, not the way users interact with their systems.

Users continue using their computers exactly as they do today.

Documents, photographs, downloads, Git repositories, and other personal data continue to evolve naturally.

The only behavioural difference should be that system updates are intentionally delayed.

* Success Criteria

** The project will be considered successful if it can:

*** Capture an exact NixOS revision.
*** Deploy that exact revision after an arbitrary observation period.
*** Preserve reproducibility.
*** Require only minimal changes to an existing modular NixOS configuration.
*** Avoid increasing day-to-day operational complexity.
*** Feel like ordinary NixOS administration with one additional concept: delayed deployment.

* Implementation Principles

Implementation details are intentionally secondary.

The implementation should satisfy the project requirements rather than define them.

** Possible implementations include:

*** A minimal flake wrapper.
*** Other mechanisms capable of preserving an immutable revision.

The implementation will be chosen according to simplicity, maintainability, and suitability—not popularity.

* Proof of Concept

The first implementation should be performed manually.

The objective is to validate the workflow rather than automate it.

Automation will only be considered after sufficient operational evidence has been collected.

* Future Work

** Potential future work includes:

*** Candidate status reporting.
*** Observation period tracking.
*** Deployment reminders.
*** Optional automation.
*** Git integration.
*** Release metadata.
*** Multi-host support.
*** Documentation and user guides.

* Acceptance Criteria

The project should be abandoned or redesigned if achieving its objectives requires unnecessary complexity or significantly alters the normal NixOS administration workflow.

The simplest implementation that faithfully satisfies the project's objectives should always be preferred.

* Project Statement

Nix-Lock captures the exact NixOS revision available at a chosen point in time, preserves that revision for a defined observation period, and intentionally deploys that exact revision later.

Its purpose is not to delay updates indiscriminately, but to separate capture from deployment while preserving the normal NixOS user experience.
